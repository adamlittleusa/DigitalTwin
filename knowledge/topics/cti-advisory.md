Adam's view on the problem he left Recorded Future to solve.

Most cyber threat intelligence teams didn't grow out of the intelligence community; they grew out of IT operations. They can buy excellent data and get it into their hands, and the platforms have largely solved the technical side of operationalizing it. What those teams often lack is the knowledge of how to build and manage intelligence operations beyond the tooling: the process, the tradecraft, the way you scale the work and make it more effective and efficient. The result is teams spending a lot of money on intelligence data and not fully operationalizing it once they have it.

Adam's answer was CTI advisory: teach CTI fundamentals, analytical skills, and advanced tradecraft, with the emphasis on bridging that operationalization gap. He built the service from scratch at Accenture, from the workshop materials and talk tracks to the pricing and delivery, and led its delivery for marquee enterprise clients.

The shape of the service: a maturity assessment first, as the input, then three tiers. Tier one is workshops and gap identification. Tier two adds a roadmap and deeper workshops. Tier three embeds the team to do the work alongside the client and leave better process behind. The topics it covers are building a team and managing intelligence operations, requirements management, analyst skills such as investigations and attribution, threat intelligence platform management and deployment, insider risk programs, and CTI maturity assessment and benchmarking. The underlying view: a threat feed is a product sale, and what most teams actually need to buy is the work of getting better at intelligence.
