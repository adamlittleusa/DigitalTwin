## Context

Adam found Recorded Future by Googling machine learning plus cybersecurity; three days later he was employee 51. He joined in July 2018 as an Intelligence Services Consultant, based in Boston, and stayed four years. The company was acquired during that time.

## What I did

Progressed from Intelligence Services Consultant to senior and principal-level consulting responsibilities, then became Product Architect for Data Science. As a consultant, partnered with customers to establish and mature cyber threat intelligence programs, translate intelligence requirements into practical workflows, and increase adoption across security teams. Advised enterprise security teams on integrating threat intelligence into investigations, threat hunting, reporting, and decision-making. As Product Architect, served as the connective layer between customers, Product, Data Science, and Software Engineering, translating real-world intelligence requirements into scalable platform capabilities and guiding security features from MVP through general availability.

His titles ran Customer Success Consultant, Senior Customer Success Consultant, Principal Customer Success Consultant and Strategic Account Advisor, then Product Architect.

On the strategic accounts side he worked with Verizon Business, which had run a lengthy evaluation of intelligence providers and chosen Recorded Future as the data backbone of a new CTI service it was bringing to market. Adam worked directly with the department leader there as his personal advisor on how to structure the service and on how the intelligence value in the Recorded Future APIs mapped onto it, and he wrote Python scripts to pull new data for them as new features came online. Separately, over the years he built deep expertise in typosquatting data, an area where Recorded Future's data was especially strong, and he was a direct analyst contributor to the sections of the Verizon Data Breach Investigations Report that dealt with typosquatting and breaches.

As Product Architect he worked closely on the acquisition and integration of SecurityTrails data into the Recorded Future intelligence graph, on the PolySwarm data integration and partnership, and on the Identity module and the Attack Surface module as they were brought to market for the first time.

## Outcomes

- Advised a portfolio representing more than $30 million in annual recurring revenue.
- Expanded CTI adoption across Verizon teams by more than 350 percent.
- Helped grow an at-risk account from $3 million to $14 million.
- Contributed research to two Verizon Data Breach Investigations Reports.
- Designed a metrics and ROI reporting service adopted by more than 150 clients.

## Stories

**The metrics project.** While managing strategic accounts, Adam kept hearing the same complaint: clients couldn't have the ROI conversation with their budget holders and CISOs. Recorded Future exposed usage metrics through its APIs, but the teams using the platform weren't engineers and couldn't pull that data themselves. Adam had just enough Python and JavaScript to do something about it, so he built a small project on the side that pulled the metrics and produced reports. Before long he was supporting well over a hundred clients with weekly and monthly reports on how they were using the platform. It became the largest tagged Jira in the company's history, and the product team built a dedicated page in the UI for the concept.

**How product found him.** That project led to the product side. Recorded Future created the product architect role for Adam, sitting between the field and the data science team and working directly with the head data scientist. He translated value from the field into what actually got built and shipped, and he evaluated novel data sets with the head data scientist for their intelligence value and what it would take to productize them. He describes it as work he loved.

**Turning an account around.** The at-risk account in the outcomes above is one Adam inherited when it was in danger of churning. He built a deep relationship with the service owner and made himself as valuable to them as he could, and the account came around and increased its investment.

**What stuck.** Two first principles Adam carries into every similar role. Learn your own product deeply: an expert on the product at a product-and-services company will always find it easier to be valuable to clients. And voice of the customer: clients will tell you what they want and need, but you have to listen deeply and respond accordingly, bring something of value to every conversation, and never be the person who shows up waiting for the client to do your job for you. He thinks of it as decision-maker support, delivered as customer service.

## Skills used

CTI program maturity consulting, enterprise advisory, strategic account advisory, requirements translation across Product, Data Science, and Engineering, metrics and ROI design, MVP-to-GA feature delivery, data set integration and partnerships, typosquatting analysis, Python and JavaScript scripting against APIs.

## Why I moved on

Through the product work Adam saw a bigger CTI problem further upstream: advisory. Teams were spending serious money on threat intelligence data, and the platform had solved getting it into their hands, but most of those teams grew out of IT operations rather than the intelligence community and didn't have the background to build and manage intelligence operations beyond the technical side. They couldn't fully operationalize what they were buying. Adam coined that CTI advisory and made the case that it was the next big problem. The company agreed it was real but it wasn't a business it wanted to build, since that is what firms like Accenture do. So he decided to be brave, jump out, and go solve it himself.
