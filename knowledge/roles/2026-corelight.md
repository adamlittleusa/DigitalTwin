## Context

Corelight is the commercialization of Zeek: network defense data from an installed sensor, which often serves as the ground truth of what is happening on a network. Adam describes network detection and response (NDR) as the often-forgotten third leg of the SecOps stool, alongside SIEM and EDR. Corelight recruited Adam to help build their AI story and AI strategy, and he joined as Principal Product Manager for AI-Driven Security Response, working remotely.

## What I did

Led product strategy and roadmaps for Model Context Protocol (MCP), ecosystem integrations, SIEM content, and developer capabilities supporting human and AI-driven security operations workflows. Engaged customers to understand their security architectures, technology stacks, data stores, and investigation processes, translated their requirements into executable priorities, and partnered with engineering and UX to deliver triage and response capabilities across SIEM, data lake, and SOAR environments. Served as a subject-matter expert in AI-enabled security operations, network evidence, NDR, and agentic investigations.

In his own telling: Corelight had spent the previous two years launching Investigator, its SaaS AI product. It was succeeding, but SaaS reached only a slice of the customer base, because most customers were sensor-only, regulated organizations that couldn't adopt a SaaS solution. Adam's job was to own the voice of the customer for that sensor-only base and turn an in-flight MCP project into something useful and productive for them. He built a design partner program, turned those conversations into a knowledge base of design intention that reshaped the company's AI product strategy outlook, got the MCP into early-adopter design partners' hands to collect feedback from that direction too, and managed the work that kept the other integrations and SIEM apps at parity with the main integration, Splunk.

## Outcomes

- Stood up a design partner program for sensor-only customers and turned it into a working knowledge base of design intention.
- Reframed the AI product strategy around one set of outcomes delivered in two flavors, SaaS and sensor-only, instead of two separate products for two separate groups.
- Got the MCP into early adopters' hands with a feedback loop, and held the integrations and SIEM apps at parity with Splunk.

## Stories

**Two flavors, not two products.** The realization Adam counts as the core lesson of the role. Applying AI and automation to security operations looks complicated and bespoke to every customer, and it does need bespoke contextualization at the level of each environment. But what customers want to achieve at the end of the day is mostly the same. Corelight's SaaS customers and its sensor-only customers were being treated as two separate things and two separate groups; what Adam saw in the design partner conversations was that they were two approaches to the same outcomes. In his words, it sounds simple in a summary after the fact, and that is part of the elegance of it, but it is not an easy realization at the ground level in the middle of the data.

Product strategy and roadmapping, voice-of-the-customer work, design partner programs, customer discovery, MCP and integration design, SIEM and SOAR content, agentic investigation workflows, integration parity management, working across engineering and UX.
